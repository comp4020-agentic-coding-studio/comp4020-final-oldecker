# the board

The board is a small, closed catch-up space for about fifteen of my
closest friends from my undergrad: a place to post short "feeling" or
"doing" updates, with a couple of photos if you want, water a shared
plant just by showing up, and react to each other's posts with a drag
from freezing to on fire.

## Who this is for

This is for the specific group of friends I did my bachelor's degree
with. We're starting to scatter — different cities, different jobs,
different daily rhythms — and the thing that used to keep us in each
other's lives without any effort, just seeing each other at uni several
times a week, is gone. I want a place that pulls us back together and
keeps us actually connected, not another group chat everyone mutes.

## What breaks without it

Without something like this, I think we'd stay loosely in touch — the
odd message, a group chat for logistics and memes — but lose the smaller,
ongoing stuff: how someone's actually doing between the big news, not
just at it. That's the gap this is meant to close.

## What "good" means, concretely

Good means the quiet people in the group post about as often as the loud
ones — not one or two people carrying the whole board while everyone else
lurks. Realistically, "everyone posts something at least once every
couple of weeks" is the floor I'm aiming for; more than that is a bonus,
not the bar.

## What's deliberately not here

There's no public visibility, on purpose. If this ever needs to reach
more people, the answer isn't opening it up — it's that someone else
makes their own closed group and invites their own friends into it.
There's no algorithm choosing what you see, and no follower counts:
nothing here is built to be grown or optimised, just used by the people
already in it.

## What I read, or didn't

I didn't go looking for precedents before building this — the decisions
here came out of reasoning about what my specific friends actually need,
not from reading about what other small-group apps do. If anything
shaped it, it was reacting against the brief's own warning about building
"a chat room with the nouns swapped" rather than any particular article
or app.

## Where this honestly stands right now

Right now I'd call this a worse blog post: you post, and the app has no
way of telling you whether a friend saw it, reacted to it, or was even on
the board at the same time as you — which, for something whose whole
point is staying connected, is more than a cosmetic gap. It's genuinely a
bit annoying already. What's missing for this to stop being that: real
feedback that a post, a watering, or a vote landed *live*, not just on
the next reload, and some visible sense of who's actually here right now.
Both are planned, not built. A collaborative drawing game is also still
just an idea, and matters less than those two for getting the core of
this right.
